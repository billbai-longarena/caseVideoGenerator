# Sales Management Case Videos · 5 Seasons, 100 Episodes Master Plan

> Five seasons are not five parallel lecture series — they form a single leadership growth staircase: Diagnose → Intervene → Design the System → Upgrade Yourself → Lead Through Chaos. A viewer who completes all five seasons has walked the entire arc from first-time manager to battle-tested leader.

---

## Progression Logic

```
Season 1  Read the Room        AMO Framework: Ability / Motivation / Opportunity
    ▼  You diagnosed it. Now what?
Season 2  Choose the Right Rx   Intervention methods and motivational mechanics
    ▼  The individual is fixed. What about the team?
Season 3  Fix the Soil          Team ecology and system design
    ▼  The team is humming. What about the manager?
Season 4  Rewrite Your OS       The manager's own evolution
    ▼  Textbook moves mastered. Now survive the real thing.
Season 5  Win Ugly              When best practices break down
```

---

## Anti-Repetition Mechanisms

### 1. Frameworks Escalate, Not Repeat

Each season owns one management altitude. The next season treats the previous one as a prerequisite. No "same lesson, different theory."

### 2. Diverse Narrative Arcs

Season 1 uses one dominant arc: **Misdiagnosis → Wake-up Call**. Seasons 2–5 introduce at least six additional story shapes:

| Arc Type | Primary Season | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Misdiagnosis → Wake-up Call | S1 | The manager read the situation wrong and reality corrected them |
| Right Diagnosis, Wrong Medicine | S2 | They saw the problem but the fix itself caused damage |
| Solved the Pain, Killed the Drive | S2 | Hygiene factors met but no motivators in sight |
| Everyone's Right but the System's Broken | S3 | Emergent dysfunction — root cause shifts from people to structure |
| Promoted but Not Upgraded | S4 | The old playbook fails at the new altitude |
| Tried Everything Normal — Time to Break the Rules | S5 | Counter-intuitive decisions under extreme pressure |
| Bright Spot — What They Got Right | Every season finale | A positive case that extracts actionable principles |

### 3. Every Block Ends with a Positive Case

Episodes 1–4 of each block show pitfalls. Episode 5 is a bright spot — a manager who got it right — so viewers leave each block with a concrete model to emulate, not just mistakes to avoid.

## Season 1: Read the Room (20 Episodes)

**Core Framework:** AMO — Ability, Motivation, Opportunity (Appelbaum et al.)
**Core Tension:** The first mistake managers make is not choosing the wrong fix — it is diagnosing the wrong problem.

> Most new sales managers default to one hammer: training (if they're patient) or pressure (if they're not). The AMO lens forces a slower, more accurate read before any action is taken.

| Block | Episodes | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| Block 1 | 1–5 | Mistaking low motivation for low ability |
| Block 2 | 6–10 | Mistaking structural barriers for attitude problems |
| Block 3 | 11–15 | When it really is a skill gap — and how most managers still handle it wrong |
| Block 4 | 16–20 | The manager's own blind spots in the AMO triangle |

### Episode Overview

**Block 1: Calling It a Skill Problem When It's a Will Problem (1–5)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Enterprise SaaS | A rep who closed $2M last year suddenly "can't sell" — manager sends her to training. Real issue: comp plan change killed her motivation |
| 2 | Medical Devices | Manager builds an elaborate onboarding playbook for a struggling hire who already knows the product cold — the rep just doesn't believe the territory is winnable |
| 3 | Commercial Real Estate | Top performer stops prospecting. Manager assumes burnout and lightens the load — actually the rep saw two peers get promoted over him and checked out |
| 4 | Logistics / 3PL | Entire team "forgets" to use the new CRM. Manager orders more training sessions. Real issue: the old CRM was built with their input; the new one was forced on them |
| 5 | Financial Services | Bright spot: A manager notices her team's pipeline drought isn't about skills — she runs stay-interviews and uncovers three hidden motivation killers |

**Block 2: Blaming People for Broken Systems (6–10)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Telecom | Reps miss follow-up windows — manager blames laziness. Root cause: the lead routing system assigns leads 48 hours late |
| 7 | Insurance | Underperformance in a newly acquired region. Manager fires two reps before discovering the territory mapping gives them 3× the drive time of peers |
| 8 | Industrial Supply | A top closer can't upsell the new product line. She's willing and capable — but pricing approval requires 5 sign-offs and buyers won't wait |
| 9 | Hospitality Tech | The whole team avoids selling to mid-market. Manager calls it "comfort zone laziness." Actual barrier: commission structure pays 2× for enterprise, making mid-market irrational |
| 10 | Renewable Energy | Bright spot: A regional VP audits "the water the fish swim in" before blaming any rep — finds three structural blockers and removes them in 30 days |

**Block 3: The Skill Gap Is Real — But You're Still Handling It Wrong (11–15)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | Cybersecurity | Rep is motivated and the system is fine — but she genuinely can't run a discovery call. Manager gives "feedback" instead of structured skill-building |
| 12 | Pharmaceutical | New hire has the knowledge but freezes in front of physicians. Manager tells him to "just be confident." No one teaches him how |
| 13 | Construction Equipment | Veteran rep can sell on relationships but can't sell on value. Manager assumes "you can't teach an old dog new tricks" and gives up |
| 14 | Ad Tech | A team of junior reps all plateau at the same stage of the sales cycle — the bottleneck is a missing skill the manager doesn't know how to teach because she's never had to articulate it |
| 15 | Manufacturing | Bright spot: A manager builds a deliberate-practice loop (observe → model → drill → debrief) and compresses ramp time by 40% |

**Block 4: The Manager's Own AMO Blind Spots (16–20)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | Automotive | The manager is willing and the system supports him — but he literally does not know how to coach. He defaults to "ride-alongs and hope" |
| 17 | Cloud Infrastructure | She diagnoses her team perfectly but can't diagnose herself — her own "opportunity" barrier is a VP who blocks every initiative |
| 18 | Consumer Packaged Goods | Manager has the skill and the access, but after two reorganizations he's lost all motivation. The team mirrors his energy and spirals |
| 19 | Professional Services | First-time manager knows she needs help but the company offers zero management training — an organizational opportunity failure |
| 20 | Fintech | Bright spot: A manager applies the AMO framework to herself first, finds her own gaps, and builds a personal development board |

## Season 2: Choose the Right Rx (20 Episodes)

**Core Frameworks:** Situational Leadership (Hersey & Blanchard) · Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan) · SBI Feedback + GROW Coaching · Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory
**Core Tension:** The manager finally reads the room correctly — but the prescription itself is harmful. A correct diagnosis is necessary, not sufficient.

| Block | Episodes | Framework | Theme |
|---|---|---|---|
| Block 5 | 21–25 | Situational Leadership | Mismatching management style to the rep's development stage |
| Block 6 | 26–30 | Self-Determination Theory | Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness — the three pillars of intrinsic drive |
| Block 7 | 31–35 | SBI Feedback + GROW Coaching | Right message, wrong delivery |
| Block 8 | 36–40 | Herzberg Two-Factor | Eliminating dissatisfaction ≠ creating motivation |

### Episode Overview

**Block 5: Style–Stage Mismatch (21–25)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | Enterprise Software | Manager micromanages a senior rep who just needs a sounding board — the rep quits inside three months |
| 22 | Luxury Retail | New hire gets "sink or swim" autonomy on day one. She sinks. Manager calls it a bad hire |
| 23 | Industrial Automation | A rep transitions from hunting to farming; his manager keeps coaching him like a hunter. Results crater in the new role |
| 24 | Healthcare IT | Manager uses the same "Monday motivation speech" for a team of 12 at wildly different stages — half feel patronized, half feel abandoned |
| 25 | Commercial Insurance | Bright spot: A manager maps each rep to the Situational Leadership grid and adjusts weekly — team hits 140% of quota |

**Block 6: Crushing Intrinsic Drive (26–30)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 26 | Tech Staffing | Manager removes all decision-making from reps to "ensure consistency." Best performers — the ones who crave autonomy — leave first |
| 27 | EdTech | Reps are given stretch goals with zero training or support. They fail, feel incompetent, and stop trying |
| 28 | Specialty Chemicals | Team works in isolation — no team meetings, no peer learning, no shared wins. Individual results are fine, but no one feels they belong, and attrition creeps up |
| 29 | Freight Brokerage | Manager adds gamification leaderboards. For the top 3, it's motivating. For the bottom 7, it's a daily public humiliation |
| 30 | Biotech | Bright spot: A manager redesigns the week so every rep has one "maker day" of self-directed work — discretionary effort jumps measurably |

**Block 7: Feedback That Backfires (31–35)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 31 | Digital Marketing Agency | Manager delivers textbook SBI feedback — but does it in front of the team. The rep hears nothing after the first sentence |
| 32 | Banking | Annual review surprises: the manager saved all critical feedback for one conversation. The rep feels ambushed, not coached |
| 33 | Semiconductor Equipment | GROW coaching session turns into an interrogation. The rep gives the "right answers" to escape, learns nothing |
| 34 | Food & Beverage | Manager gives only positive feedback for six months to "build trust." When the real conversation finally comes, credibility is already spent |
| 35 | Aerospace | Bright spot: A manager uses real-time, bite-sized SBI feedback tied to specific deals — reps call it the most useful input they've ever received |

**Block 8: The Hygiene Trap (36–40)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 36 | Retail Banking | Manager lobbies for a massive pay raise. Complaints drop — but discretionary effort doesn't budge. The team is no longer dissatisfied, but they're not motivated either |
| 37 | Facilities Management | New office, flexible hours, free lunch. Glassdoor score improves. Revenue stays flat. Manager is baffled |
| 38 | Telecommunications | Manager fixes every "hygiene" complaint on the engagement survey. Next survey: satisfaction up, engagement down. Different constructs entirely |
| 39 | Legal Tech | A competitor offers 15% higher base. Instead of matching, a smart VP invests in recognition, career pathing, and meaningful work — and retention improves |
| 40 | Managed Services | Bright spot: A manager separates "stop the bleeding" (hygiene) from "light the fire" (motivators) and runs parallel playbooks for each |

## Season 3: Fix the Soil (20 Episodes)

**Core Frameworks:** Psychological Safety (Edmondson) · Incentive Game Theory (Goodhart's Law, Cobra Effect) · Team Dynamics (Tuckman, Lencioni) · Systems Thinking (Senge, Meadows)
**Core Tension:** The manager keeps staring at individuals, but the problem lives in the space between people — in relationships, rules, and feedback loops. Many "people problems" are actually system problems.

| Block | Episodes | Framework | Theme |
|---|---|---|---|
| Block 9 | 41–45 | Psychological Safety | The invisible wall — teams that can't speak up, experiment, or admit mistakes |
| Block 10 | 46–50 | Game Theory + Goodhart's Law + Cobra Effect | Rational actors gaming rational rules — unintended consequences of incentive design |
| Block 11 | 51–55 | Tuckman Stages + Lencioni Five Dysfunctions | Different team stages need different management |
| Block 12 | 56–60 | Iceberg Model + System Feedback Loops | Structural problems beneath surface events |

### Episode Overview

**Block 9: Psychological Safety (41–45)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 41 | Pharmaceutical Sales | Every pipeline review is green. Q4 arrives and half the deals evaporate — reps were hiding bad news because the last person who flagged a risk got publicly grilled |
| 42 | Venture-Backed Startup | A junior rep proposes a creative outbound strategy. Manager dismisses it in a team meeting. No one pitches an idea again for six months |
| 43 | Chemical Distribution | Team never does deal post-mortems — because the last one turned into a blame session. Same mistakes repeat every quarter |
| 44 | Defense Contracting | Manager proudly says "my door is always open." But everyone who walked through it ended up on a PIP within 90 days |
| 45 | Healthcare Staffing | Bright spot: A manager institutes "pre-mortems" and blameless retros. Her team starts surfacing risks early, and forecast accuracy jumps 30 points |

**Block 10: The Incentive Trap (46–50)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 46 | Real Estate Brokerage | Stack ranking creates a zero-sum war: agents hoard leads, sabotage co-listings, and the brokerage's referral culture dies |
| 47 | Online Education | KPI is "demo-to-close rate." Reps start cherry-picking only high-intent leads and ignoring top-of-funnel entirely |
| 48 | Telecommunications | Team-based bonus pool: top performer resents "carrying the bottom half" and requests a transfer |
| 49 | Heavy Equipment | Quarterly sprint bonus creates a predictable rhythm: reps sandbag deals in month one and dump them in month three. Pipeline reporting becomes fiction |
| 50 | Agricultural Inputs | Bright spot: One small rule change — from individual ranking to shared territory milestones — turns a zero-sum team into a collaborative one |

**Block 11: Team Dynamics (51–55)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 51 | Series-B SaaS Startup | New team is fighting constantly. Manager panics and tries to squash conflict. It's actually healthy Storming — and suppressing it freezes the team in dysfunction |
| 52 | Beverage Distribution | Mature team hasn't grown in three years. They're comfortable, they like each other, they've stopped challenging each other — terminal Norming |
| 53 | Semiconductor | Star closer leaves. Team immediately regresses from Performing to Forming — their processes were actually one person's habits |
| 54 | Restaurant Supply Chain | Two acquired teams merge under one manager. He plays peacekeeper, and the real conflict goes underground and festers |
| 55 | Wealth Management | Bright spot: A manager inherits a low-trust team and accelerates through Storming with structured, facilitated conflict |

**Block 12: Systems Thinking (56–60)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 56 | Life Insurance | The same pipeline coverage problem recurs every quarter. The manager keeps solving the symptom; the structure keeps reproducing it |
| 57 | Auto Dealership Network | Fixing attrition in one region causes attrition in the adjacent region — they were sharing a talent pool and no one mapped the dependency |
| 58 | B2B SaaS | Success → complacency → declining effort → lost deals → panic — a reinforcing loop nobody noticed until it was a crisis |
| 59 | Electronics Manufacturing | Quick fixes (hiring contractors, offering SPIFs) keep papering over a broken onboarding process — a classic "shifting the burden" archetype |
| 60 | Property Management | Bright spot: A director learns to draw causal loop diagrams and changes her entire approach to recurring problems |

## Season 4: Rewrite Your OS (20 Episodes)

**Core Frameworks:** Leadership Pipeline (Charan, Drotter, Noel) · Management Leverage (Andy Grove) · Managing Up & Leading Laterally · Managerial Self-Awareness
**Core Tension:** The team is running well, but the manager's own operating system hasn't kept up. They've become the bottleneck.

| Block | Episodes | Framework | Theme |
|---|---|---|---|
| Block 13 | 61–65 | Leadership Pipeline | Every promotion is a role reset |
| Block 14 | 66–70 | Management Leverage (High Output Management) | Where you spend your time determines your impact |
| Block 15 | 71–75 | Managing Up + Leading Laterally | You don't only manage down |
| Block 16 | 76–80 | Managerial Self-Awareness | Overplayed strengths, blind spots, and stress behaviors |

### Episode Overview

**Block 13: Leadership Pipeline (61–65)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 61 | Medical Devices | The #1 rep becomes the worst manager — still trying to close deals instead of developing closers |
| 62 | Consumer Electronics | Director promoted from manager but still manages ICs. Her actual job — managing managers — goes undone |
| 63 | Enterprise Software | "She's too valuable to promote" — the organization protects one role and blocks the entire pipeline above it |
| 64 | Retail Chain | Rocket-ship promotion: a 28-year-old VP with the title of a leader two levels up and the instincts of a frontline manager |
| 65 | Logistics | Bright spot: What a successful role transition actually looks like — the mindset shifts, the skill swaps, the time-allocation reset |

**Block 14: Management Leverage (66–70)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 66 | Advertising / Media | Four hours of meetings a day, none of which change any outcome. The manager is busy but not impactful |
| 67 | CPG / FMCG | 60% of coaching time goes to the bottom 10%. The movable middle — the 60% most likely to improve — gets nothing |
| 68 | Commercial Real Estate | One 30-minute 1:1 with a struggling rep outperforms ten group rallies. The manager learns the power of high-leverage moments |
| 69 | E-Commerce | Manager does everything herself. Team output = her personal ceiling. She's the lid on her own organization |
| 70 | Energy | Bright spot: A manager shifts from "firefighter" to "fire preventer" — what changes when you move from reactive to proactive |

**Block 15: Managing Up and Leading Laterally (71–75)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 71 | Telecommunications | VP sets an impossible number. Manager accepts it silently. The team pays the price of a conversation their leader didn't have |
| 72 | Supply Chain / Distribution | Cross-functional dependency bottlenecks every deal. Manager's response: "We're waiting on Product." For eight months |
| 73 | Industrial Manufacturing | Manager fights for her team's resources with data, not drama — managing up as constructive challenge |
| 74 | Tech / Growth Stage | Caught between an aggressive CEO and an exhausted team, the manager becomes a messenger instead of a translator. Both sides lose trust |
| 75 | Consumer Brands | Bright spot: Building a cross-departmental coalition to push a strategic initiative — influence without authority |

**Block 16: Managerial Self-Awareness (76–80)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 76 | Investment Banking | The "decisive, hard-charging" manager: hero in tailwinds, tyrant in headwinds. Same trait, different weather |
| 77 | Higher Education | The "warm, consensus-building" manager: great culture, but nobody fears missing a deadline |
| 78 | Tech Scale-Up | Impostor syndrome — manager secretly feels unqualified and over-compensates: micromanaging, avoiding hard calls, or deferring to the loudest voice |
| 79 | Pharmaceutical | 360-degree feedback reveals two different people: who the manager thinks she is and who the team actually experiences |
| 80 | Agribusiness | Bright spot: Finding your own management style instead of imitating someone else's — and the performance unlock that follows |

## Season 5: Win Ugly (20 Episodes)

**Core Frameworks:** Change Management (Kotter, Kübler-Ross curve) · Inclusive & Cross-Cultural Leadership · Scaling Transitions · Counterintuitive Decision-Making
**Core Tension:** Standard methods aren't enough for extreme conditions. The ultimate test of leadership is not steady-state operations — it is navigating uncertainty, contradiction, and ambiguity.

| Block | Episodes | Framework | Theme |
|---|---|---|---|
| Block 17 | 81–85 | Kotter's 8 Steps + Change Curve | Leading a team through the valley of uncertainty |
| Block 18 | 86–90 | Inclusive Leadership + Cross-Cultural Management | Difference is a resource, not a problem |
| Block 19 | 91–95 | Scale Transitions + Organizational Transformation | What works at 10 people breaks at 100 |
| Block 20 | 96–100 | Counterintuitive Decisions | Why the "wrong" move sometimes works |

### Episode Overview

**Block 17: Leading Through Change (81–85)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 81 | Brick-and-Mortar Retail | Digital transformation rollout. Veteran reps resist en masse — their fear isn't the new tool, it's obsolescence |
| 82 | Real Estate | Post-layoff survivor syndrome: the people who stayed lost trust too. "If it happened to them, I'm next" |
| 83 | Manufacturing | Corporate announces a major strategic pivot. The frontline manager doesn't believe in it herself but has to lead the charge |
| 84 | Education Services | Regulatory shift zeroes out the legacy product line overnight. The team must "start from zero" with the same people |
| 85 | Travel & Hospitality | Bright spot: In the middle of a crisis a manager does one thing right — stabilize the humans before optimizing the process |

**Block 18: Leading Across Difference (86–90)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 86 | FMCG / Consumer Goods | A Boomer director manages a Gen-Z sales floor. Their definitions of "hard work," "loyalty," and "feedback" are incompatible — until the director stops translating and starts listening |
| 87 | Cross-Border E-Commerce | HQ team (low-context, direct) clashes with regional team (high-context, relationship-first). Both think the other side is unprofessional |
| 88 | Technology | Technical sellers vs. relationship sellers on the same team — mutual contempt dressed up as "culture difference" |
| 89 | Management Consulting | Fully remote team across four time zones. Manager can't "see" effort, defaults to surveillance tools, and trust collapses |
| 90 | Consumer Products | Bright spot: A deliberately diverse "misfit" team outperforms the homogeneous A-team. How the manager made it work |

**Block 19: Scale Breaks Everything (91–95)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 91 | Venture-Backed SaaS | Founder-CEO is still personally closing deals at 50 employees. He's the company's #1 rep and its #1 bottleneck |
| 92 | Franchise / Multi-Location | The culture that traveled by word-of-mouth dies at location #20. You need process, but you're terrified of losing the soul |
| 93 | DTC E-Commerce | From "hero selling" to "system output" — de-heroifying the sales org is necessary and deeply painful for the heroes |
| 94 | National Real Estate | Regional expansion: the manager can no longer be in every room. The "cloning yourself" problem |
| 95 | Industrial Distribution | Bright spot: An organization's deliberate shift from "hunting" culture to "farming" culture — and the year-two payoff |

**Block 20: Counterintuitive (96–100)**

| Ep | Industry | Story Core |
|---|---|---|
| 96 | High-Growth Tech | Firing the top performer — and the team explodes with productivity because the toxic drag is gone |
| 97 | Insurance | Deliberately lowering the quota — and revenue goes up because reps stop gaming and start selling |
| 98 | Creative Agency | Adding constraints (fewer verticals, smaller deal sizes) — reps are forced into creativity and deal quality improves |
| 99 | Restaurant Group | Manager "disappears" for two weeks. Self-organization activates. The team discovers capability they didn't know they had |
| 100 | Cross-Industry | Five-season retrospective: one manager's complete journey from "Read the Room" to "Win Ugly" — and a map for the viewer's own path forward |

## Industry Coverage

Across five seasons, 40+ distinct industries appear. When the same industry recurs in a later season, it focuses on a different management altitude. No industry repeats the same framework dimension.

**Industry Distribution Principles:**
- Mix of B2B and B2C across every season
- At least 30% of episodes feature industries recognizable to non-US audiences (cross-border, manufacturing, agriculture, hospitality)
- No single vertical appears in more than 3 seasons
- Season 1 industries that recur must shift to a new framework lens
